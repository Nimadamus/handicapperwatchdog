# BayAreaSportsBlog article draft (owner approval required before publishing)

Headline: Why a 70% Winner Can Still Lose Money: What 52,000 Graded Picks Say About Odds

Dek: A win rate means nothing until you put it next to the price. Here is what the numbers show when public handicapper picks are graded at the odds they actually took.

---

Every sports bettor has seen the pitch. A handicapper is "hitting 70%," so the picks must be worth following. The problem is that a win rate on its own leaves out the most important number in betting: the price.

Handicapper Watchdog grades picks that handicappers post in public, on X, Reddit, Covers, TheRX and other forums. Each pick is captured before the game starts and graded on the final score at the price the handicapper posted, one unit per pick. As of November 3, 2026, that adds up to more than 52,000 graded picks with a posted price. Grouping them by the odds taken shows how much the price decides.

**Heavy favorites win a lot and still lose.** Picks at minus 200 or shorter won 71.0% of the time. That sounds elite until you do the math: at those prices a bettor needs to win 73.9% just to break even. The result was a return of minus 3.9%. The minus 150 to minus 199 range told the same story, winning 59.0% against 62.4% needed, a return of minus 5.3%.

**The standard price is close to a coin flip with a fee.** At the usual spread and total prices, minus 105 to minus 120, this group won 53.5% against 52.7% needed, a return of plus 1.6%.

**Modest underdogs did best.** Picks from plus 121 to plus 149 won only 46.5% of the time, but they needed just 43.1%, and returned plus 7.7%.

The account level picture is even clearer. Among the 104 accounts with at least 100 priced picks, five won 52.4% or more of their picks and were still down money. Of the accounts that beat the win rate their own prices required, 95% were profitable. Of the accounts that did not, none were.

## What to ask about any record

Next time someone advertises a win rate, ask two questions. What prices were those picks at? And over how many picks? A 70% record built on minus 300 favorites can be a losing record. A 45% record built on plus 150 underdogs can be a winning one.

## What we are confident about, and what is still being checked

The findings above come from picks with a posted price between minus 100 and plus 249, graded on final scores, from accounts whose full history reproduces their published record. Those are the reliable observations.

Two things are not settled, and we are leaving them out rather than guessing.

First, very long prices. Picks at plus 250 or longer are excluded. When we compared every long price with the market price at the moment it was posted, many did not match the bet at all. A player prop like "Darnell Washington +300" had been read as a bet on the Washington Huskies. A national title future had been read as a single game moneyline. On November 3, 2026, 278 picks priced at plus 150 or longer were held out of every public record for this reason. Their original posts and grades are preserved, and nothing has been regraded. Picks in leagues with no market price to compare against are still being checked.

Second, the sample. These are handicappers who post in public, not all bettors, and the results describe the past. They are not a prediction and not betting advice.

The full tables, charts and method are published at [Handicapper Watchdog](https://handicapperwatchdog.com/research/odds-and-profit.html).
